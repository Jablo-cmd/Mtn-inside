# MTN Konect — Product Concept and Business Outcomes

**Tagline:** Connect. Belong. Konect.  
**Brand sign-off:** Be MTN.  
**Status:** Proposal and prototype concept only. Not an official MTN product, approved initiative or production service.

## 1. Executive summary

MTN Konect is a proposed digital employee experience platform for connecting MTN employees across countries, teams and functions. It brings together an employee community, official communications, career development, recognition, innovation, events and approved MTN product campaigns.

The goal is to create a place employees choose to use while giving MTN a measurable, governed channel for employee connection and selected business activities. Konect must complement, integrate with or clearly improve on existing MTN systems; it must not duplicate them without a demonstrated reason.

The current GitHub application is a concept prototype. Sample content and simulated interactions must not be mistaken for live MTN data or production functionality. MTN authorisation, secure employee authentication, security review, approved integrations, operational ownership and a pilot are prerequisites to real deployment.

## 2. The idea in one sentence

**MTN Konect is a proposed global employee platform where MTN people connect, communicate, grow, recognise one another, contribute ideas and discover approved MTN products and campaigns—with measurable outcomes for employees and the business.**

## 3. The problem it is intended to address

The concept is designed to investigate and potentially address these challenges:

- Employees across markets and functions may have limited opportunities to connect beyond their immediate teams.
- Company announcements and resources can be distributed across different tools and channels.
- Employees may not easily discover internal career opportunities, mentors, learning resources or colleagues with useful expertise.
- Employee ideas and feedback may lack a visible route from submission to review and implementation.
- Employees may not consistently discover approved product launches and offers relevant to their market.
- Leaders may lack a consolidated view of engagement and outcomes across these activities.

These are hypotheses to validate with MTN employees and stakeholders, not claims that MTN currently lacks tools or processes for them. Discovery must map existing MTN platforms, identify gaps and determine whether Konect should complement, integrate with or replace any specific experience.

## 4. Who it is for

### Primary users
- MTN employees in participating countries, business units and functions.
- Authorised employees and administrators who publish official content.
- Approved teams responsible for HR, internal communications, learning, events, innovation, employee experience and product campaigns.

### Access boundaries
- Eligibility must be defined by MTN. Contractor access requires explicit policy approval.
- Public registration must not be enabled for an employee-only service.
- Oracle or other employee identifiers must never be exposed publicly or used as visible profile identifiers.
- Users must only access information and functions permitted by their role, country and approved policies.

## 5. Product principles

1. **Employee value first:** the community must be useful and welcoming, not merely an advertising channel.
2. **Global community, relevant local experiences:** support cross-market connection while respecting country-specific content, language, rules and offers.
3. **Clear distinction between official and employee content:** official announcements and promotions must be visibly labelled and published only by authorised users.
4. **Privacy and safety by design:** collect the minimum data required, restrict access, protect identities and provide moderation and reporting.
5. **Evidence over assumptions:** measure outcomes against a baseline and do not claim success from activity counts alone.
6. **Complement existing MTN systems:** investigate current platforms before deciding what Konect should own or integrate.
7. **Accessible and mobile-friendly:** design for varied devices, connectivity conditions, accessibility needs and languages.
8. **No unsupported promises:** planned capabilities must be clearly distinguished from working prototype features.

## 6. Proposed product areas

### 6.1 Community feed
A moderated employee community for constructive conversations, questions, personal stories, interests, hobbies, culture, music, sports, wellbeing, recipes, technology, achievements and other appropriate topics.

**Intended outcome:** more opportunities for employees to connect across teams and markets.

**Requirements:** posts, comments, reactions, topic labels, reporting, moderation, privacy controls and accessible mobile layouts. Respectful disagreement and criticism should be permitted. Harassment, threats, hate, doxxing and disclosure of confidential information must be handled under approved rules.

### 6.2 Official announcements and notices
A dedicated area for authorised company and country-level announcements, policy updates, operational notices and internal news.

**Intended outcome:** make important communications easier to find and measure.

**Requirements:** authorised publisher roles, approval workflows where required, audience targeting, publication and expiry dates, official labels, search and audit history. Konect must not be treated as the authoritative channel for critical notices until MTN approves its operating model.

### 6.3 MTN products and offers
A space for authorised MTN teams to share approved products, services, devices, launches and promotions with relevant employee audiences.

**Intended outcome:** improve product awareness and test whether internal campaigns generate qualified engagement or attributable commercial outcomes.

**Requirements:** market and eligibility labels, accurate prices and terms, expiry dates, campaign owner, approved links and creative assets, appropriate notification preferences and campaign analytics. Employee participation in sharing campaigns must be voluntary. Employee reach, clicks or likes must not be presented as sales.

### 6.4 Careers and growth
A space for approved internal vacancies, mobility opportunities, mentoring, learning programmes and professional knowledge-sharing.

**Intended outcome:** improve discovery of internal development opportunities and make expertise easier to find.

**Requirements:** verified vacancy sources, country and eligibility rules, links to official recruitment systems, role-based access and a clear statement that Konect does not itself make hiring decisions. It must not bypass recruitment policy.

### 6.5 Recognition and appreciation
A way for employees to recognise colleagues for teamwork, customer focus, mentoring, innovation and other MTN-approved values.

**Intended outcome:** make contributions visible and encourage appreciation across teams.

**Requirements:** transparent rules, safeguards against abuse, moderation, optional visibility and a clear distinction between peer appreciation and formal performance or reward decisions.

### 6.6 Ideas and innovation
A structured place for approved business challenges and employee suggestions, with a visible review and delivery process.

**Intended outcome:** turn employee ideas into assessable proposals and, where worthwhile, implemented improvements.

**Requirements:** challenge owners, submission guidance, review criteria, status tracking, decision rationale, intellectual-property terms and escalation paths. Do not solicit confidential, customer-sensitive or security-sensitive information in an uncontrolled forum.

### 6.7 Events and activities
A calendar for town halls, learning sessions, employee events, volunteering and local community activities.

**Intended outcome:** make activities easier to discover and participation easier to coordinate.

**Requirements:** time zones, local relevance, registration and capacity controls where needed, reminders, cancellation updates and post-event feedback.

### 6.8 Communities and groups
Employee groups based on country, professional practice, language, culture, hobbies, sports and shared interests.

**Intended outcome:** support meaningful connections outside reporting lines and immediate teams.

**Requirements:** clear group ownership, privacy options, group moderation, discoverability controls and community standards.

### 6.9 Employee knowledge assistant — future phase
A possible AI assistant that answers employee questions using approved, current company sources and links back to those sources.

**Intended outcome:** reduce time spent locating information and help employees navigate approved resources.

**Requirements before launch:** approved source connectors, identity-aware permissions, citations to source documents, freshness controls, logging, privacy review, accuracy evaluation, escalation when uncertain and a clear statement that generated answers may be wrong. It must not expose data the user is not authorised to see.

### 6.10 Leadership impact dashboard
A governed dashboard showing aggregate adoption, communication engagement, campaign performance, participation in growth activities and innovation outcomes.

**Intended outcome:** help sponsors assess usage, outcomes, costs, risks and whether to continue or expand the pilot.

**Requirements:** role-based access, documented metric definitions, privacy-safe aggregation, data-quality checks and limits on individual-level employee monitoring. Engagement data must not be used as a proxy for employee performance.

## 7. What results must MTN Konect achieve?

The pilot must establish baseline measurements before launch and agree targets with the sponsor. The following are proposed metrics, not promised results.

| Outcome | How to measure it | Evidence of value |
|---|---|---|
| Employee adoption | Eligible users who activate; monthly active users; repeat use | Employees return and use meaningful features |
| Communication effectiveness | Reach, views, acknowledgement where appropriate, comprehension checks | Important content reaches the intended audience and is understood |
| Cross-team connection | Participation across functions and markets; voluntary user feedback | Employees report discovering useful contacts and communities |
| Career discovery | Click-throughs to verified opportunities; mentoring and learning participation | Employees discover relevant approved development routes |
| Recognition | Recognition participation and recipient feedback | Recognition is meaningful, fair and broadly accessible |
| Innovation | Ideas reviewed, decisions made, ideas trialled and implemented | A traceable path from idea to action, with benefits documented |
| Product campaigns | Eligible audience reach, clicks, qualified referrals and attributable conversions | Campaign performance is assessed beyond impressions |
| Operational efficiency | Time saved measured against a documented baseline | Verified time savings exceed the costs of the relevant process |
| Trust and safety | Reports, response times, repeat incidents, privacy/security events | Issues are handled under clear rules and serious incidents escalated |
| Financial viability | Build, integration, hosting, support and operating costs versus validated benefits | Sponsor can make an informed continue/stop/expand decision |

Targets should be agreed after discovery and baseline collection. Do not invent adoption, savings, revenue or engagement targets without evidence.

## 8. Intended impact

### For employees
- Easier access to relevant updates, communities, colleagues and approved opportunities.
- More ways to share interests, celebrate contributions and learn from one another.
- A clearer route to submit ideas and understand what happens to them.
- A respectful and inclusive space with meaningful privacy and safety controls.

### For MTN teams
- A possible common layer for selected employee experiences.
- More measurable distribution of official information and approved campaigns.
- Better visibility of employee ideas and cross-team knowledge-sharing.
- Data to assess whether the platform is useful and cost-effective.

### For MTN as a business
- Potential improvement in employee connection and communication effectiveness.
- Potentially better discovery of internal talent and learning opportunities.
- A testable channel for approved product campaigns and employee advocacy.
- A structured way to capture and evaluate employee-generated improvements.
- Evidence to guide investment decisions instead of relying on anecdotal claims.

These are intended impacts, not guaranteed outcomes. They depend on employee adoption, good governance, integration quality, relevant content and demonstrated incremental benefit.

## 9. What success looks like

A successful pilot would demonstrate all of the following:

1. The platform addresses a validated employee or business need not already adequately served by existing MTN systems.
2. Eligible employees can securely sign in and access only permitted content.
3. Employees use the platform repeatedly for useful activities, not just a launch-day visit.
4. Authorised teams can publish and manage official content with clear accountability.
5. At least one selected business use case—such as communication effectiveness, innovation or product-campaign measurement—shows credible results against an agreed baseline.
6. Employees understand the privacy rules and trust the reporting and moderation process.
7. Total operating and support costs are known well enough for an informed investment decision.
8. The sponsor receives a documented recommendation to stop, revise, continue or expand.

## 10. Scope boundaries

### Potentially in scope
- Employee community and groups.
- Official announcements and events.
- Approved product campaigns.
- Careers, mentoring and learning discovery.
- Recognition and structured innovation.
- Aggregate analytics.
- Approved integrations and an employee knowledge assistant in later phases.

### Out of scope unless separately approved
- Replacing HR, payroll, recruitment, identity, learning, intranet or customer systems without an explicit decision.
- Publishing unapproved vacancies, promotions, prices or company announcements.
- Public access to an employee-only platform.
- Exposing employee numbers, personal information or confidential company content.
- Making employment, promotion, disciplinary or performance decisions using engagement scores.
- Claiming direct sales, retention, productivity or financial gains without valid evidence.
- Using AI to access restricted company documents or make consequential decisions without explicit governance.

## 11. Security, privacy and governance requirements

Before real users or company information are onboarded, MTN and the delivery team must define:

- Executive sponsor, product owner, technical owner and operating budget.
- Eligible user groups and identity provider; approved single sign-on and account lifecycle.
- Role-based access and market-specific permissions.
- Data inventory, purpose, lawful basis, retention and deletion rules.
- POPIA and any applicable country-specific privacy requirements.
- Security architecture, threat modelling, secure development, vulnerability testing and incident response.
- Moderation policies, reporting and appeals, escalation routes and response expectations.
- Approval workflows and accountability for official announcements and product promotions.
- Vendor, hosting, data residency, integration and contractual requirements.
- Accessibility, language, support, service availability, backup and recovery expectations.
- AI governance and source permissions if AI features are introduced.

Do not use real employee identifiers, production credentials, confidential data or internal systems in the public prototype. Any connection to MTN systems requires explicit authorisation.

## 12. Pilot proposal

### Stage 1 — Discovery
Interview representative employees and stakeholders from HR, internal communications, IT/security, product marketing and relevant existing platform teams. Map existing tools, workflows, pain points, constraints and data sources.

**Deliverable:** validated problem statement, gap analysis, initial requirements and a decision on whether Konect should proceed.

### Stage 2 — Prototype review
Demonstrate the concept with clearly fictional sample data. Gather feedback on navigation, accessibility, useful features, privacy expectations and existing-system overlap.

**Deliverable:** prioritised user feedback and revised prototype. No production data.

### Stage 3 — Pilot design and approval
Select one bounded use case, eligible group, duration, owner, budget, baseline metrics, target measures and success criteria. Complete architecture, security, privacy and legal reviews.

**Deliverable:** signed-off pilot plan and go/no-go decision.

### Stage 4 — Controlled implementation
Build approved authentication, permissions, data handling, moderation, audit logging, integrations and analytics. Test security, accessibility, reliability and support procedures before onboarding pilot users.

**Deliverable:** pilot-ready service that has passed agreed acceptance criteria.

### Stage 5 — Evaluation
Compare results against the baseline; assess user feedback, outcomes, costs, risks and unresolved issues.

**Deliverable:** documented stop, revise, continue or expand recommendation, supported by evidence.

No launch date, pilot duration, budget or adoption target should be treated as agreed until the sponsor and delivery team approve it.

## 13. Commercial and operating model

A potential delivery model is a sponsored discovery followed by a paid pilot and, if successful, a phased rollout. Commercial terms must be discussed with MTN and should account for product development, integrations, hosting, security testing, support, moderation, maintenance and future enhancements.

Possible models include:
- MTN funds and owns a custom internal platform.
- MTN sponsors a pilot with agreed licensing and support terms.
- Konect integrates with existing MTN systems, with ownership and maintenance responsibilities defined contractually.

No model is assumed to be accepted. Intellectual property, source code ownership, licensing, data ownership, service levels, security responsibilities and exit/transfer arrangements must be agreed in writing before production delivery.

## 14. Known status of the current prototype

The GitHub repository is Jablo-cmd/Mtn-inside; the application branding has been changed to **MTN Konect**. The prototype is intended to communicate the concept and demonstrate possible user experiences.

The current single-file web prototype is not a production platform. Sample posts, notices and users may be fictional, and browser-local interactions do not provide shared multi-user functionality. No live MTN identity, HR, product, communications or analytics systems should be assumed connected. Proposed features described in this document may not yet exist in working code.

## 15. Key risks and mitigations

| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| Duplicates existing MTN platforms | Complete discovery and integration mapping before committing to build |
| Employees do not adopt it | Co-design with employees; test usefulness; remove friction and irrelevant notifications |
| Becomes an advertising feed | Keep community value central; label promotions; control frequency and targeting |
| Incorrect or unauthorised official content | Verified publishers, approval workflows, audit history and expiry controls |
| Privacy or security incident | Security-by-design, least privilege, testing, incident response and approved data handling |
| Misleading impact claims | Baselines, defined metrics, attribution methods and transparent reporting |
| AI returns incorrect or restricted information | Approved sources, permission-aware retrieval, citations, evaluation and escalation |
| Costs exceed benefits | Bounded pilot, full cost accounting and explicit stop/expand criteria |
| Branding or name not authorised | Obtain MTN approval and complete trademark/legal checks before public launch |

## 16. Decisions required from MTN

Before implementation beyond a concept demo, MTN needs to determine:

1. Is there a validated problem Konect should solve?
2. Which existing platforms already address it?
3. Who is the executive sponsor and accountable product owner?
4. Which employee group and country should be considered for a pilot?
5. Which single business use case should be tested first?
6. What data, identity systems and integrations are authorised?
7. What security, privacy, legal, procurement and brand approvals are required?
8. What budget and operating model are acceptable?
9. Which baseline metrics and success thresholds will determine the pilot outcome?
10. Who owns the product, code, data, support and future development?

## 17. Final definition

**MTN Konect is a proposed global employee experience platform built to help MTN people connect, belong, develop, recognise contributions and share ideas, while giving MTN a secure and measurable way to communicate with employees and test approved business initiatives.**

Its purpose is not to add another app for the sake of having one. Its purpose is to solve a validated problem, improve the employee experience, produce measurable business outcomes and earn further investment through evidence.

**The governing rule:** build only what employees need and MTN can authorise, secure, operate and measure.
